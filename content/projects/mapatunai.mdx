## The problem

A road or bridge can be fully paid for on paper and still be bare dirt on the ground. Checking a public works project means piecing together the official record, the contractor behind it and what is actually there, and citizens rarely have any of that in one place.

## What we built

MapaTunAI ("Mapping What's Real") is a civic transparency platform that tests every public works project against **three layers of evidence**:

- **What the government claims.** DPWH project records: contract amounts, reported completion, source of funds and official inspection updates.
- **What citizens experience.** Ground-level reports and ratings from community members, verified with phone OTP and geotagged site photos to keep out spam.
- **What satellites prove.** ESRI Wayback before-and-after imagery and 360° Google Street View, so anyone can see whether a project was actually built.

On top of that:

- **A national map** of more than 240,000 public works contracts across 18 regions, clustered on satellite, dark and terrain layers, with region filters, search and a "near me" view.
- **Regional anomaly risk ratings** that show how many projects in each region are flagged.
- **A contractor accountability registry** that ranks contractors by award value, completion rate and overdue projects, and shows joint-venture patterns.
- **An AI audit:** Gemini cross-checks a project's records, satellite imagery and citizen reports to flag irregularities.

## How it works

DPWH project data, PSA regions and provinces, contractors and citizen reports live in PostgreSQL on Supabase, accessed through Prisma and served by a 26-endpoint Next.js API. The front end renders the map with Mapbox GL, pulls historical imagery from ESRI Wayback, and embeds Street View for on-the-ground checks. Gemini runs the audit, comparing what each layer says about the same project.
